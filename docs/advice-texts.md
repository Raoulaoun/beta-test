# Advice texts (for approval)

Every fixed text the tool shows: metric advice for the PDF plan and the day-2 email, red flag explanations, headline insights and the three education emails. Approve or edit inline.

## Rules for all advice texts

Every text coaches the process, never the market. It talks to a trader as a future manager.

- Advice on risk, sizing, consistency and habits only. Never instruments, directions, entries or signals.
- No promise of results and no mention of Beta admission.
- Short: a one-line diagnosis, then two or three concrete actions.
- Each metric text uses the trader's own number in place of `{value}`.
- Tone: direct and respectful, like a senior trader talking to a junior one.

## Metric advice: risk control

**Max drawdown**

Your deepest drop was {value}. An investor who saw that drop live would most likely have withdrawn.

- Set a hard monthly loss limit, for example 6% of the account, and stop trading for the month when you hit it.
- Cut position size by half after every 5% drop from your peak, and only restore it after a new high.
- Treat a 10% drawdown as your outer wall: that is where most investors start asking questions.

**Worst day**

Your worst single day cost {value} of the account. One day should never be able to damage months of work.

- Set a daily loss limit, around 2%, and close the platform when it is reached.
- Avoid stacking several positions on the same theme in one session; they tend to lose together.
- Be flat or reduced before major news releases.

**Average loss per trade**

Your average losing trade costs {value} of the account. Professionals keep this small enough that a losing streak is boring, not dangerous.

- Decide your risk in money before every trade, then size the position from the stop distance.
- Aim for about 1% risk per trade or less.
- Use the position-size calculator until this becomes automatic.

**Stop loss usage**

Only {value} of your trades had a stop loss. An investor's first question is "what happens if you're wrong?", and a stop is the answer.

- Place the stop with the entry, every time, before the trade can move against you.
- Never widen a stop once the trade is open.
- If a trade has no logical stop level, it is not a trade.

## Metric advice: discipline

**Revenge sizing**

After a loss, your position size rises to {value} your normal size. Trading bigger to win it back is the most common path to a blown account.

- Keep the same size for the rest of the day after any loss.
- After two losses in a row, stop for the session.
- Write your size rule down and check every order against it.

**Adding to losers**

{value} of your positions were added to while already in loss. Averaging down turns a small mistake into a large one.

- Add only to positions that are in profit and moving your way, or not at all.
- Define your full position size before the first entry.
- If the idea is wrong, close it; don't fund it.

**Holding losers**

You hold losing trades {value} longer than winners. Hoping a loser comes back while cutting winners early drains an account slowly.

- Let the stop decide when a loser ends, not your feelings.
- Set a time limit: if a trade hasn't worked within your normal holding time, close it.
- Use a target or trailing stop on winners instead of closing them early.

**Overtrading**

On {value} of your trading days, you traded far more than usual. Bursts of activity usually come from emotion, not opportunity.

- Set a maximum number of trades per day, for example your normal count plus two.
- Trade from a written plan made before the session opens.
- On a quiet day, no trade is a valid result.

## Metric advice: consistency

**Profitable months**

{value} of your months were profitable. Investors stay with managers who deliver steady months, not occasional big ones.

- Review every losing month: was it the market, or a break from your rules?
- Lower your risk in the second half of a month that is already down.
- Judge yourself by the month, not the trade.

**Profit from a few trades**

{value} of your net profit came from your 5 best trades. Without them, the record looks very different, and investors will notice.

- Look for an edge that repeats, not one big win.
- Check that your rules would take those 5 trades again; if not, they were luck.
- Grow the number of solid, ordinary winners rather than chasing home runs.

**Best month**

Your best month makes up {value} of your total profit. One great month cannot carry a track record.

- Keep risk constant from month to month, so results reflect skill, not bet size.
- After a big month, don't raise size; protect the gain.
- Aim for results spread evenly across the whole record.

## Metric advice: risk-adjusted return

**Return compared with drawdown**

Your yearly return is {value} times your max drawdown. Investors ask how much pain they must sit through for each unit of gain.

- Lowering drawdown is usually easier than raising returns, and it improves this number just as much.
- Cut the setups that produce your largest losses before adding new ones.
- Target a yearly return at least twice your max drawdown.

**Sortino ratio**

Your Sortino ratio is {value}. It measures return against harmful volatility: the down days.

- Reduce the size and frequency of bad days with a daily loss limit.
- Stay out of trades during conditions where your losses cluster.
- Smooth, steady gains raise this score more than big spikes.

**Profit factor**

Your profit factor is {value}: for every $1 lost, you made that much back. Below 1.3, small costs and bad luck can erase your edge.

- Cut losers faster: keep the average loss smaller than the average win.
- Drop the setups that lose money over time; your record shows which ones.
- Include commissions and swaps when you judge a strategy.

## Metric advice: track record depth

**Months of history**

Your record covers {value} months. Investors trust what has survived different market conditions.

- Keep trading the same account with the same rules; a long, unbroken record is an asset.
- Don't reset or open new accounts to hide a bad period.
- Retest every few months: depth only grows with time.

**Number of trades**

Your record has {value} closed trades. With few trades, it's hard to tell skill from luck.

- Keep following your rules; the sample grows on its own.
- Log every trade in the same account so the full record stays in one place.
- Around 300 trades, a record starts to speak for itself.

**Recent activity**

Your last trade was {value} days ago. Investors want to see current skill, not a past one.

- Upload a recent statement that includes your latest trading.
- If you took a break, resume with reduced size and rebuild a recent record.
- Retest once you have at least 3 months of recent trading.

## Red flag explanations

Shown on page 3 of the PDF. Each says what was found, why investors reject it, and the way out.

**Martingale**

We found chains where position size grew sharply after each loss. This can look profitable for months, then wipe out an account in a single bad run. No serious investor allocates to it, whatever the past returns. The way out: fixed risk per trade, the same size after a loss as before it.

**Grid**

We found several positions opened in the same direction at worse and worse prices. A grid hides losses until the market keeps going one way, then they arrive all at once. The way out: one planned position per idea, with a stop for the whole position.

**Blown or near-blown account**

This account lost {value} from its peak at some point. Recovering from a drop that deep takes more than doubling what's left, and investors see it as proof that risk was not under control. The way out: rebuild a clean record on a fresh account with strict loss limits, then retest.

**No stop losses**

Almost none of your trades had a stop loss. Without one, a single trade can decide the fate of the account, and an investor can't know their worst case. The way out: no order goes in without a stop.

## Education emails (Developing band)

Sent on days 2, 5 and 10 to traders scoring under 40. Each teaches one habit and links one free calculator. Every email ends with the standard disclaimer footer.

### Email 1, day 2: Risk first

**Subject:** The one number every manager knows before a trade

Hi {first\_name},

Thanks for taking the Manager Test. Your full report is in the email we sent right after the test.

Here's the habit that separates managers from traders: they know exactly how much they can lose before they enter a trade. Not roughly. Exactly.

It works like this:

1. Decide the most you'll risk on one trade: around 1% of the account.
2. Place your stop where the idea is proven wrong.
3. Let the distance to the stop decide the position size, not the other way around.

Do this on every trade and one bad day can no longer break your account. Our free position-size calculator does the math for you: {calculator\_link}

The Beta team

### Email 2, day 5: Consistency beats big wins

**Subject:** Why investors prefer boring months

Hi {first\_name},

When an investor looks at a track record, the first thing they check isn't the total return. It's how the return was made.

A record built on two huge months scares them, because it might never happen again. A record of steady, ordinary months reassures them, because it looks repeatable.

Three ways to build that kind of record:

1. Keep the same risk per trade every month, win or lose.
2. Stop raising your size after a great month.
3. Review each losing month and ask: did I follow my rules?

See how fast losses compound against you with our drawdown recovery calculator: {calculator\_link}

The Beta team

### Email 3, day 10: Rules over emotions

**Subject:** The trade after a loss is the most dangerous one

Hi {first\_name},

Most blown accounts don't die from one bad trade. They die from the trades that come after it: bigger size to win it back, adding to a loser, one more trade than planned.

Managers protect themselves with simple written rules:

1. After a loss, the next trade uses the same size. Never bigger.
2. After two losses in a row, the session is over.
3. No adding to a position that's losing.

Write these down and keep them next to your screen for the next 90 days. Then take the Manager Test again and see what changed: {retest\_link}

The Beta team
